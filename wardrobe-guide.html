The Wardrobe Guide

Storage, hangers, moths and repair: what happens to clothes during the long stretches when you are not wearing them.

A garment spends perhaps a hundred hours a year being worn and the rest of the time hanging in the dark. How it is stored decides whether it is still wearable in five years, and it costs almost nothing to get right.

Hang or fold

Woven garments hang: shirts, jackets, coats, trousers and dresses. Knitted garments fold: jumpers, cardigans, T-shirts and anything with stretch. The reason is simple. A knit is a structure of loops, and gravity pulls those loops into a longer, narrower shape that will not come back.

Hangers matter more than they sound

Wire hangers put the entire weight of a garment onto two small points, which is what creates those permanent bumps in the shoulders. Use broad wooden or padded hangers for jackets and coats, shaped plastic for shirts, and clip hangers for trousers if you hang them by the hem rather than folding them over a bar.

Give things room

A rail packed solid creases everything on it and stops air moving, which is how damp and mustiness start. Leave a finger’s width between hangers. If the rail will not allow that, the honest answer is that there are more clothes in it than the space can keep in good condition.

Moths

Clothes moths eat protein fibres: wool, cashmere, silk and feathers. They are drawn to the traces we leave behind rather than to the wool itself, which is why the single most effective step is washing or cleaning wool before it goes away for the summer. Store clean knitwear in sealed boxes or bags with cedar, and check the back corners of wardrobes twice a year.

Moths go for the dirty jumper at the back, not the clean one at the front. Wash before storing and the problem mostly disappears.

Seasonal rotation

Twice a year, take out everything for the season that is ending. Wash or clean it, repair anything that needs it, and store it in breathable cotton bags or boxes rather than plastic, which traps moisture. Keep the boxes somewhere dry and dark; lofts swing between extremes of temperature and damp.

Repair while it is small

A loose button takes two minutes. A split seam takes ten. A hole in a knit takes an afternoon and never looks quite the same. Keep a small kit of matching thread, spare buttons and a darning needle, and deal with things the week you notice them rather than putting the garment back in the wardrobe to get worse.

What to let go

Storage is not free, in space or in condition. Anything you have not worn in two full cycles of the season is unlikely to be worn again. Passing it on while it is still in good order is better for it than another three years in the dark, and it gives the clothes you do wear the room they need. When you are deciding, the care label and the Care Symbol Reader will tell you honestly how much work keeping it will be.
